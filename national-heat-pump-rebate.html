Published amounts

What the government page says a household may receive

The Government of Canada page says households can be eligible for a rebate of $2,000 or up to $10,000 for low- to median-income households. Actual rebate amounts will depend on household income and the cost of the equipment installed. These are federal program figures. They are a government description of possible rebate levels, and they are separate from Vortex’s installation charge.

Dates published so far

Timing and retroactivity

The government page says the National Heat Pump Rebate will be available through an online portal in early 2027, with no energy audit required before or after installation. It also says the program is expected to launch in early 2027 and close in March 2034. The Government of Canada describes the wider effort as a $2 billion investment to support up to 820,000 households choosing a cold-climate heat pump. That is the government’s description of the program, and each household still has to qualify and be approved.

Retroactivity period

Published retroactivity criteria

The list below follows the government page’s section “To be eligible for a Rebate during the retroactivity period.” Eligibility will be assessed on the program requirements in effect on the date the heat pump was installed. The page also says the rebate supports eligible heat pump systems that can heat the entire home and are installed by HVAC contractors.

  • The heat pump is on the eligible product list (opens in a new tab), identified by its Air-Conditioning, Heating and Refrigeration Institute (AHRI) number or numbers.
  • The heat pump was purchased from and installed by an HVAC contractor.
  • You reside in Canada and own the home you are applying for.
    • The home does not need to be your primary residence. The government page gives a property you rent out as an example.
    • If you reside in an Indigenous community and the home is owned by the local Indigenous government, the government page says you meet this ownership criterion.
  • Federal funding has not previously been provided for a heat pump at the address you are applying for.
  • Only one rebate is available per household, even if you own more than one property.
  • During the retroactivity period, the government page encourages you to confirm with your contractor that the system is appropriately sized to heat your whole home.
  • Emergency and planned installations are both accepted. The page says you do not need to remove the existing system or demonstrate that it has broken down.

Heating fuel. The rebate is described for households who currently heat with electricity, including electric furnaces or electric baseboards, natural gas, heating oil, propane, diesel, coal, and/or wood. It is a multi-fuel description on the federal page.

Contractor registration. The published retroactivity criteria require purchase from and installation by an HVAC contractor. They do not list a separate contractor-registration step. Final rules are still pending, so this page treats registration as unresolved. Vortex is an HVAC contractor that installs heat pumps. This page makes no federal registration, authorization, or approval claim for that work.

Bills and fit. The government page tells homeowners to consult their installer about whether a heat pump makes sense locally. It says that for households currently heating with natural gas, switching to a heat pump may not decrease heating bills, depending on region, the system selected, and other factors. It also notes that certified installers and maintenance can be limited in more remote areas. Vortex’s published service area is Peterborough County, Kawartha Lakes, and Durham Region. Vortex does not promise a lower energy bill, a dollar amount of savings, or a particular brand or installation price on this page.

Paperwork

What the receipt needs to show

The government page says you must submit your receipts within 6 months, and that the contractor receipt needs the items below. The government page does not say when that 6-month period starts. The application portal is still closed. Keep the paperwork, and use the government’s instruction once the portal explains the timing. This page does not choose a start date for that clock.

  • The name and address of the person who will apply for the rebate.
  • The name of the company installing the heat pump.
  • The make, model, and AHRI number of the installed equipment.
  • The number of heat pumps installed.
  • The total cost for the installation project, including necessary directly related work such as ductwork and electrical upgrades.

Ask the installing contractor to put those items on the receipt. For a Vortex installation, ask Vortex to include them on the invoice for that job. The applicant sends the application and the receipts to the program. A complete-looking invoice is paperwork for that application. Acceptance is decided when the government reviews it.

Higher rebate tier

Income thresholds

To receive the higher rebate amount (up to $10,000), the government page says your after-tax household income must be equal to or less than the amount for your province or territory and household size. The amounts section also describes that higher tier as below-median income, and the introduction describes it as low- to median-income. Use the official table and the government’s instructions together. Vortex does not decide which tier a household is in.

The government page tells applicants to calculate after-tax household income from the 2025 income tax return: line 23600 (net income) minus line 43500 (total taxes payable), do the same for a spouse or common-law partner, and add the two results. That method is for the government application. It is not a tax service from Vortex, and this website does not collect it.

That is the trade credential already stated on the homepage. It is separate from the federal rebate, and this page does not turn it into a program registration.

  • The application, on the federal portal expected in early 2027.

Still unfinished

Rules can still change

  • Program parameters and eligibility criteria are still being finalized and remain subject to change.
  • Eligibility is assessed using the requirements in effect on the installation date.
  • A model’s place on the eligible product list can change. Check the AHRI number against the current list before you treat equipment as eligible.
  • Rented or leased heat pumps are described as possibly eligible only after the official launch, with details still to come.
  • Funding is not guaranteed until an application is approved. No payment is made before launch and approval.

From the government page

Scam warning

The Government of Canada says salespeople have been visiting and calling homes to sell heat pumps with misleading references to this rebate. It says Environment and Climate Change Canada does not go door to door to inspect, sell, or rent heating and cooling equipment, and that it is not making marketing calls. The page asks you to be the person who starts the conversation with an HVAC company.

Vortex is a contractor you can call yourself. Vortex is not the National Heat Pump Rebate, and Vortex does not send anyone to your door on the government’s behalf.

If someone at your door claims to represent the National Heat Pump Rebate, the government page asks you to alert Environment and Climate Change Canada, contact local police, and report it to a provincial, territorial, or federal consumer protection office. The government address published for that alert is PNRT-NHPR@ec.gc.ca. Consumer-protection contacts linked from the official page are listed under sources.
